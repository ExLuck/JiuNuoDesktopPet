# Character guide

This page is the official place where character scope, data boundaries, asset provenance and character phase progress are maintained. General features are in [the feature guide](FEATURES.md), and on-device procedures are in [the manual test checklist](../SELF_TEST.md).

## Adopted character direction (2026-09-07)

Feibi Jiubi does not eat or lick icons, has no hunger bar, and uses her own mischief bar above her head. The code isolates character capabilities through `SupportsFood` and `UsesMischief`: Feibi does not start the deletion and icon consumption watchers, does not join the feeding queue, does not choose hunger behaviour, and keeps no food lines or food animation entry points, while Nuonuo keeps the existing feeding gameplay. The fasting toggle still belongs to Nuonuo and is no substitute for character isolation.

The adopted gameplay is ordinary kicking, punching, charging and hat collection of windows, then throwing the hat once the bar is full to perform the burst kick that smashes a window or the two-handed tear, after which she picks the hat up, puts the real snapshot's shards or halves into the hat opening, wears it again and returns to her day; the window is kept until a right-click restores it. The complete design and safety boundaries are in [the overall window mischief plan](FEIBI_WINDOW_PLAY_PLAN.md). Code being wired up does not mean the assets and the desktop effect have been accepted; the final section of this page is where current progress is maintained. The pancake flatten, the paper plane and the crumple-and-shoot remain later candidates.

## Adopted scope and switching rules

Nuonuo or Feibi Jiubi is selected inside the existing WPF character window, reusing the physics, terrain, dragging, sleep and general settings, with optional coexistence of both pets and interactions between them. Mischief snapshots and the hat use transparent effect windows, and window results are kept according to the rules in the next section; autonomously chasing, dodging, following or hugging the mouse stays removed. Switching characters does not restart the process.

The tray's "Pet character" lists both characters. The tick, the tooltip name, the size label and the last choice are updated only after a successful load; `SetCharacter` only synchronises the display. A switch request during dragging, during Nuonuo's swallow, or during the current satisfied animation is held and applied at a safe boundary; clicking the currently ticked character again cancels a pending switch request. Switching to Feibi clears the old food queue and the fake icons without handing the pending eating task to Feibi or adding a meal count, while Nuonuo's completed feeding records are kept. A switch requested during Feibi's mischief cancels the interaction first and tries to return any window still stored by the pet.

The old character's progression data is saved before switching, and the new character's animations, voice service and save are prepared; where frames are missing or preparation fails, the old character is kept. At startup, if the last chosen non-default character cannot be loaded, it falls back to Nuonuo. Where a non-default character has no readable tray icon, the system application icon is used rather than Nuonuo's character icon.

## Characters and data boundaries

### Window results are restored by the user (adopted)

After the hat storing, the smash or the tear finishes performing, the real window stays minimized. Once a finisher's attack is done she picks the hat up and holds it steady, using the existing `hat_store` native frames to put the shards or halves into the hat opening one at a time within 3 seconds, then wears the hat and returns to her day; once that is done there are no window shards or leftover cards on the desktop. There is no countdown return and no automatic restore of the complete window. It is taken out only when the user right-clicks the character or the tray and chooses "Give the window back": the stored destruction result comes out of the current hat opening first, then reassembles at its original position, and she puts the hat back on once the real window is confirmed restored. Clicking the hat or the shards does not trigger a return, and the effects pass the mouse through while held, so they never block desktop work.

`PrankEffectWindow` stores the final draw transform for every real snapshot slice, and storing only appends the arc towards the hat opening, the shrink and the brim clipping, never replacing a shard with a complete card. `SetCollectionProgress` is separate from the original destruction progress, and `IsCollected` does not mean the system has restored anything; after being taken back out in reverse, it still reassembles through the original `Shatter` or `Tear` type and the original snapshot. That local path serves only the two existing finishers, is maintained by the project maintainer, and adds no assets and no general animation framework.

The tear first pulls the complete snapshot taut vertically, then splits it into whole upper and lower pieces along a single horizontal jagged rupture, with no sideways wipe crop. There is no crack before the rupture, and afterwards each piece's grip point follows the upper and lower hand respectively, freezing at the maximum spread once released. `bare_tear`'s real current character frames are drawn in front of the snapshot while the original character image is hidden, to avoid covering her face or leaving a semi-transparent ghost; the hand anchors and the foreground both use the character visual's actual screen transform, including the temporary body size and the squash and stretch. The hat actions continue to use the verified hat-opening foreground mask. The existing frame sequences and anchors are unchanged, with no image regenerated or edited.

The temporary body size used only for tearing reuses the existing WPF size and body squash and stretch: after throwing the hat she grows over 0.8 seconds, with the target referencing both the user's body size and the window height, normally about 2.25-3 times her original size and capped at 70% of the work area's short side; a character who is already large is never shrunk for the performance. `Core/TearPrankPresentation.cs` maintains this local size and pose rule, and `MainWindow.Tear.cs` handles the sole anchoring, staying on screen, growing and shrinking back. It does not call `ChangePetSize`, which would save a preference, and does not alter the user's size setting; it shrinks back smoothly after the release on frame 10, and completion, interruption, character switching and display changes all clean up the temporary size. That path serves only the existing tear, is maintained by the project maintainer, and no general transformation system is extracted from it.

### Single-slot window storage and concurrent small actions

The hat holds at most one window result, but having something stored no longer prevents kicking, punching or charging another compatible window; the "Play a prank" menu item stays available, and re-entry during a performance is still prevented by the character state. While the hat is occupied, no second window is hidden inside it and no new smash or tear requiring the hat is launched; a full bar stays ready and ordinary small mischief carries on. Ordinary idling, dragging, going home, size changes, quiet mode, turning autonomous mischief off and switching to another application all keep the stored window, overwriting and automatically returning nothing.

`MainWindow.PrankStorage.cs` uses lazy single-slot storage: when a performance finishes, the original `WindowPrankController` still holds its own window and in-memory snapshot. Only when the next kick, punch or charge is about to start is that complete controller parked in the storage slot along with its target, snapshot, action type, contact point and hat-opening state, and a separate controller created for the current small action. It reuses the existing ownership checks, `IsHeld` / `HoldSnapshot()` and the native return evidence, copies no snapshot, and never hands the old result to a controller that is currently moving a different window. An ordinary small action being refused, cancelled or failing ends only that action and never returns the window in the hat as a side effect; the stored window continues to pass its safety checks independently.

When the user chooses "Give the window back", the current small action is cancelled first, then the stored window's original controller, snapshot, action type and contact point are reconnected, and that same window is returned along its original complete-card or shards-and-halves path. `WindowPrankHoldPolicy` maintains the rules for whether an interruption must return the stored window. This design serves only Feibi's "one stored result plus one current small action", is maintained by the project maintainer, adds no multi-slot inventory framework, and persists no window handle or screenshot. `PrankStorageParked` and `PrankStorageReturnRequested` record parking and retrieval, and are no substitute for accepting the real window behaviour.

A safe exit, switching characters, locking or sleeping, and a real display layout or work area change all still clean up the current small action and try to return the stored window; where the stored window's own identity or safety check fails, the original ownership rules apply. When the user restores, closes or takes over the stored target from the taskbar, its ownership is released immediately and it is never re-minimized; operating a different small action's target does not count as taking over the stored one. The pet crossing screens, or DPI adaptation, does not trigger a return. A crash cannot guarantee an automatic return, but the window itself is not closed and can still be restored from the taskbar. Snapshots live only in memory and are never written to disk or uploaded; the state log records the handle, the phase, whether it is held and the return result, and never the window content. `prank.snapshot.held` and `PrankCompleted.AwaitingManualRestore` mark the waiting state, `PrankRemainsStored` marks the shards having been stored, and `PrankManualRestoreCompleted` marks the user's return having completed.

Character IDs, runtime directories and rhythm parameters have `Core/PetCharacterProfile.cs` as their code entry point; at this stage there are only these two real consumers, using immutable configuration and the existing state machine, with no plugin protocol and no general character framework. The project maintainer is responsible for keeping the configuration, the action contract, the voice mapping and this page's record in step.

### Both pets together and synced settings (2026-09-23 adopted, awaiting on-device acceptance)

The official everyday entry point adds a persistent "**Both pets together**" toggle. Once enabled, the current character keeps her original window and the other character appears in her own transparent window nearby, on the monitor the current character is on; the two windows run their own animations, physics, needs and character-specific features separately within one process, sharing a single tray and right-click menu and one set of settings. Turning coexistence off ends the companion character and saves her own separate save; choosing either character from "Pet character" also switches back to single mode. The official edition's single-instance lock and the separate entry points of the feature-test and Behavior Control editions are unchanged. The implementation is in `MainWindow.Coexistence.cs`, `MainWindow.xaml.cs` and `TrayIconService.cs`, serves only the two existing characters, and is maintained by the project maintainer.

The size slider, mute, quiet mode, "Bring her home" and infinite mode all apply to both at once. Fasting changes only Nuonuo's hunger logic; autonomous mischief and returning windows are performed only by Feibi, though the coexistence menu shows both sets of character-specific entries. Infinite mode is a new persistent option: both characters' hunger and mischief bars are hidden, Nuonuo's hunger is pinned at zero and she stops begging, licking icons and the hungry roll, while Feibi's mischief build-up, cooldowns and window safety conditions still run by their original rules. Turning infinite mode off restores the separately configured fasting mode and does not reset Feibi's mischief value. Each character still saves her own life and mischief state, with no merged save; the two-pet actions and the icon relay are in [two-pet interactions](INTERACTION_PLAN.md).

2026-09-28, adopted progress bar sync rules: while coexisting, if either fasting or infinite mode is on, both characters' bars are hidden, and they are shown together only when both are off. After leaving coexistence, a solo Nuonuo still obeys fasting and infinite modes, while a solo Feibi's mischief bar is hidden by infinite mode only, unaffected by a retained fasting preference. This reuses the existing shared settings and `PetModePolicy`, adding no separate toggle and migrating no save; only visibility is synced, values are not merged, and Feibi's build-up and cooldowns are unchanged. Once the menu writes the shared setting both are refreshed immediately, and starting or stopping coexistence and switching characters recalculate it too; the refresh never calls the mischief simulation, so toggling a progress bar cannot trigger an extra action. This rule and the `ProgressDisplayVisibilityChanged` diagnostic are maintained by this project, and automated regressions cover the mode combinations, the switching order and restarting with either character as the primary; the desktop effect is accepted manually by the user.

### Right-click menu interaction pause (2026-09-08 adopted)

The character and the tray reuse the same menu. `TrayIconService.MenuInteractionStrip` notifies `IsMenuOpen` and `MenuOpenChanged` synchronously when the root menu opens; while it is open the character simulation, frame changes, walking and terrain scanning do not advance, while the window ownership safety check every 200 milliseconds is retained. Closing, cancelling an open, or releasing the menu all reset it; opening the character submenu does not resume walking, and right-clicking repeatedly does not call Show repeatedly. `MainWindow.Menu.cs` resets the frame clock and the platform motion sampling on close, to stop catch-up frames fast-forwarding and to stop window movement during the menu being mistaken for a throwing force. It modifies no character state, user setting or save, and the menu commands still execute.

`WindowPrankController.CheckSafety()` is separate from motion advancement, and the foreground HWND of our own menu is not treated as switching to an external application; a real target being taken over, closed, restored, or hitting a safety error is still handled by the existing rules. New `PetMenuOpened` and `PetMenuClosed` logs record the action and how long it was open, and `UiFrameSlow` records interface frame work over 100 milliseconds along with the state before and after the switch and the number of assets loaded, at most once every 5 seconds, never logging window content. This choice reuses the menu and the animation cache and isolates only interaction from continuous movement, migrating no UI framework; the project maintainer owns that lifecycle. The existing logs cannot prove a single root cause, and whether the menu's on-device responsiveness has improved still needs the user to retest.

Nuonuo keeps hunger, fullness, sleepiness, curiosity and feeding statistics. Feibi reuses sleepiness and curiosity and their bounded offline evolution, while hunger and fullness in an older save are zeroed on load and at runtime, and the historical food fields take no part in behaviour and no longer grow. The mischief state saves its value and remaining cooldown separately, and saves no window handle, snapshot or pending action; nothing accumulates offline. Quiet mode allows only autonomous idling and sleep, stopping performances while keeping a confirmed stored window, which is restored by the window holding rules.

### Autonomous mischief defaults and rhythm

The user has approved autonomous mischief being on by default, including for existing installations. `PetSettings` defaults to `Version=5` and `AllowMischief=true`; `SettingsStore.Load()` updates any configuration below version 5 to enabled once and saves it, while other existing preferences are kept by their own migration rules. If the user then switches it off in the menu, loading at v5 or later never turns it back on. Mute, character selection and size are unaffected by this v5 migration; `FeatureDemoSession` still only reads the real presentation preferences, explicitly disables autonomous mischief in its own temporary v5 settings, and neither triggers nor saves a real configuration migration.

`Core/MischiefController.cs` is the code entry point for the rhythm parameters, and this section is the official place where the corresponding product rules are maintained:

| Item | Adopted rule |
| --- | --- |
| Autonomous bar fill | Going from 0 to 100 needs 300 seconds of genuine active time; mischief performances, sleep, quiet mode, a locked screen, the menu pause and offline time do not count as valid fill time; walking and dragging can still build up, and while dragging it waits for the user to let go before considering a strike |
| Ordinary autonomous mischief | Tries once after a random 60-120 seconds; the interval is drawn again afterwards, and 6 mischief points are no longer deducted |
| Finisher | A full bar produces exactly one intent; after it completes the bar resets and cools down for 120 seconds, and cannot re-trigger consecutively |
| No suitable target | Checks again after 10 seconds; a full bar stays at 100, does not drop back because there is no window, and does not widen the target safety range |
| Something in the hat | No second window storing or finisher is triggered; a full bar waits for the hat to free up, while kicking, punching and charging other windows continue on the ordinary rhythm |
| Teasing and turning autonomy off | Each genuine tease adds 12 points, at least 1.5 seconds apart; with autonomy off, manual teasing and mischief still work and no automatic action is queued |
| A genuine interruption | Distinguished from waiting with no target; an interrupted performance still keeps the protection of at most 85 points and a minimum 90-second cooldown, and a pause or going offline stores no burst intent |

These are durations of valid time and intervals between attempts, and they do not guarantee that a safe target happens to be available at any given wall-clock time. The bar does not fill during a finisher's cooldown; the user can still trigger small mischief manually, which does not bypass that cooldown. Sleep, quiet mode, a locked screen and a rendering gap over 5 seconds accumulate no burst; an ordinary restart loads only the value and the remaining cooldown, and does not restore an old pending event.

Feibi's menu keeps the "Allow autonomous mischief" toggle and adds a non-clickable "Mischief" row explaining whether it is off, building up, cooling down, waiting for a suitable window, blocked by an occupied hat, or currently paused and why; while dragging it shows "Building up · let go of her", and an unfilled value is rounded down so 99.7 is never displayed as 100. When off, the bar above her head turns grey and does not pulse, and it returns to blue-purple once re-enabled, with no bubble announcing the value. `MainWindow.MischiefStatus.cs` chooses the status text, and `TrayIconService.SetMischiefStatus` updates only the display text that changed, continuing to use the original menu pause lifecycle and the size slider. The `MischiefStatus` log records the reason, the bar value, the cooldown and the next attempt time on state changes and at a limited rate, never logging the target's content. This status display and the parameter tuning apply to Feibi only, are maintained by the project maintainer, and how often it is disruptive on real hardware still needs the user to retest.

## Temperament and voice

Feibi Jiubi keeps the original image's big white hat, pale blonde long hair, purple eyes, blue-black-white clothes and two little feet. Her liveliness comes from actively pushing off the ground, the rebound on landing, the inertia of the hat brim and hair, and small reactions that match the scene.

- Her movement speed is `1.15` times Nuonuo's base value, and her maximum standing idle time is 4 seconds, while Nuonuo's remains 5.
- With plenty of energy she leans towards exploring and jumping; when sleepy her movement weighting drops and she still sleeps. Having no support, action cooldowns and the life modes all take priority over temperament.
- Idling, walking, running, curiosity, climbing, jumping and landing use selective rhythm adjustments, and sleep keeps a readable duration; mischief sets the wind-up and rebound times per action rather than speeding anything up globally.
- Both characters share the optional speech bubble toggle, off by default; once on they reuse their own dialogue catalogues to show short status responses, and with both pets idle they take turns talking. Test prompts and the mischief bar value still stay in the original menu and bar positions and never go into a bubble. The details are defined by [two-pet interactions](INTERACTION_PLAN.md#speech-bubbles).
- While standing idle, Feibi tries to play one of her everyday recordings every 20 to 50 seconds, subject to mute, quiet mode, the cooldown and the repeat protection; switching characters stops the previous character's sound. Nuonuo does not gain this timed everyday voice behaviour.
- Both characters use their own recordings with `HasVoice=true`; switching characters stops and releases the old player first, then uses the new character's directory and mapping. Mute and volume settings are shared, and neither borrows the other's recordings, synthesized speech or notification sounds.

`Core/FeibiVoiceCatalog.cs` is the official place where the recording mapping is maintained. The everyday pool holds the 9 numbered clips plus "normal", "quiet calm muttering" and "Feibi Jiubi", chosen at random on startup or switching in, on being brought home, on unmuting, and during standing idle chatter outside quiet mode. The original "Feibi Jiubi falling" file is kept as-is but is no longer called by any situation, so 24 of the current 25 original files are used for playback. Sleep still uses "sleepy", dragging or losing grip still uses the aggrieved set, exploration still uses "puzzled", and the separate short response for an obvious landing keeps its original mapping. Hat hiding leans towards whispering and playful softness, kicking, punching and charging lean towards positive and excited, tearing uses "gritted teeth", and the ending uses the playful set. Kicks, punches and the finishers respond from the wind-up; no extra attack recording is played on approach, which would otherwise let a short approach phase eat into the tear's response interval.

This reuses the existing `.NET 8 WPF MediaPlayer` and `VoicePlaybackService`, adding no audio library, network call or transcoding dependency; [the WPF multimedia documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/desktop/wpf/graphics-multimedia/multimedia-overview) is the official reference for that existing player. The service adds character directory routing and mischief situation entry points, and continues to be the project maintainer's responsibility. Only one recording plays at a time, keeping priority interruption and the 12-second cross-situation repeat protection for the same recording in ordinary situations; ordinary mischief has a 10-second same-situation cooldown and at least 3.5 seconds after any other voice line, and a continuous animation phase may skip a sound. The fixed full-bar opening is an exception, described below. Quiet mode plays neither the new everyday chatter nor the mischief lines, while existing reactions such as sleepiness and the user dragging her are still governed by the mute toggle. Cancelling the current mischief stops that scene's voice, and mute stops sound immediately.

Provenance and waveform auditing are recorded together in `assets/characters/feibijiubi/previews/voice-audit.json`, with `tools/audit_character_voice.py` as the review tool. All 25 source and project hashes agree, totalling 4,055,284 bytes and 28.136 seconds, all 48 kHz, mono, 24-bit extensible PCM. The binary check found no corruption, fully silent files, duplicates or near-full-scale clipping; `041` and "dejected soft" have lower amplitude, which preserves the original dynamics rather than being normalized automatically. No recording was played or uploaded for this check, and a format check is no substitute for human acceptance of how it sounds.

The opening of a full-bar big mischief always uses the user-specified `菲比啾比_菲八啾比.wav`, whose source and existing project file both have SHA-256 `D4E687696790B103A73A8710D7EC78ED7BB84FDAA2C130E608974192254D5135`, with no re-recording or transcoding. Once a safe target is found and this big mischief is confirmed able to start, the `BurstCall` phase plays that recording, and only on receiving this player's actual finished event does it move on to approaching, throwing the hat and attacking; the target is neither moved nor minimized during that wait, and is checked again afterwards. This opening is never filtered out by the random pool or the chatter cooldown, takes priority in ending any previous chatter line, and never overlaps audio; the existing cooldowns for the everyday and numbered falling pools are unchanged.

`VoicePlaybackService.PlayBurstCallAsync` reuses the WPF `MediaPlayer`, creating a separate player for that single opening so old voice completion events are isolated, and closing it as soon as it finishes, adding no audio dependency; it is maintained by the project maintainer. With mute on, volume at zero, or playback failing, the sound is skipped and the action continues, and after a cancellation, exit or character switch the old action must not continue; an open or playback timeout stops that sound rather than waiting indefinitely. The full-bar opening does not override the autonomous mischief toggle, quiet mode or the safe target restrictions. The phase logs are `VoiceBurstStarted`, `VoiceBurstFinished` and `PrankBurstCallFinished`, and how it sounds and whether she really does "finish the line before striking" are still for the user to accept.

## Action and geometry contract

Feibi's runtime requires 33 action directories, each with exactly 16 native frames, for 528 in total, named `frame_00.png` through `frame_15.png`. Nuonuo remains at the original 17 directories and 272 frames. Feibi's directories are:

```text
idle, run, walk, toss, fall, land, curious, sleep, climb, drag, jump, slide, roll
prank_sneak, kick, punch, charge, recoil, hat_open, hat_store, hat_hold,
hat_return, gloat, hat_throw, bare_idle, bare_run, bare_kick, bare_tear,
bare_recover, bare_drag, bare_fall, hat_pickup, hat_wear
```

The first 8 frames of `sleep` are for lying down and, reversed, for getting up, and the last 8 are for the lying breathing loop; `toss` uses the first 8 frames.
